# Red flags: call the family now

This list decides how fast a person is told. It is not medical advice. When in doubt, choose the higher severity and let a person decide.

## RED: possible emergency (page everyone; tell {call_name} to call {ambulance} or {emergency})
- Chest pain, pressure or tightness; pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back; cold sweat with it.
- Trouble breathing, or she can't finish a sentence.
- Stroke signs (BE FAST): loss of Balance, Eyesight changes, Face drooping, Arm or leg weakness, Speech slurred or confused. Time matters.
- A fall, especially if she can't get up, hit her head, or is in pain.
- Fainting, not waking up properly, a fit or seizure.
- Vomiting blood, or bleeding that won't stop.
- Blood pressure at or above 180/120 (hypertensive crisis range).
- Kanglish and Kannada she might use: "ede novu" (chest pain), "usiru kattide" (can't breathe), "bidde" (I fell), "ಎದೆ ನೋವು", "ಉಸಿರು", "ಬಿದ್ದೆ".

## HIGH: someone should call her now
- A scam in progress (see `hearth-scam-patterns`).
- Blood sugar below 70 or above 300.
- She sounds confused or very unlike herself.

## MEDIUM: someone should hear about it today
- Fever, dizziness ("talesuttu"), vomiting, diarrhoea, not eating, very weak.
- A missed dose, or medicines running out ("maatre mugidide").
- Pain that is new, severe, or keeps coming back.
- No reply to a check-in for two hours.
- BP at or above 160/100, or below 90 systolic.

## LOW: for the digest
- Mild pain she has mentioned before, a normal reading, poor sleep, feeling low or lonely.
